# 2026-08-11

* **Creation**: Added [Exam format](/reference/exam-format.md), [Write a practice exam](/how-to/write-a-practice-exam.md) and `<leader>ge`. A practice exam is one note and the note is the whole exam: the questions, the options and the answers all live in it, the way a todo line carries every field a todo has. Nothing marks a note as an exam, there being no frontmatter to set and no folder to use; a note holding a question heading with lettered options under it is one and a note holding neither is not, which is the bargain `parseTodo` makes with a line. The format was read off the four Claude certification exams already in the vault rather than imposed on them, and then loosened everywhere loosening was free, because those are one exam series among however many come next: `Question` or `Q`, any heading level, an answer under the question or in a key at the back, `Correct` or `Answer`, an em-dash or a hyphen, bold options or bare ones, and any heading at all as the section a question is scored under. A question whose answer is not a letter cannot be asked and is left out, and the pane says how many rather than letting a 58 question sitting look like the whole note. Sitting one writes a note in a folder beside the exam holding the score, the score per section and every missed question with the rationale, so the folder becomes a record of getting better; the exam note is never written to.
* **Creation**: Added [The archive](/explanation/the-archive.md), [KASTEN_ARCHIVE_PATH](/reference/configuration.md#kasten_archive_path) and `<leader>a`. `98 Archive` is an ordinary folder and moving a note into it is the rename key and a new path; nothing runs when it happens, and dropping the schema leaves it exactly where it was. Four lookups leave it out and one key puts it back into all four, rather than a filter term on each, which would be four spellings to remember and no answer at all for the file tree. Search and the todo list skip it in the `rg` pass rather than in the browser, because `GET /api/search` caps what it answers with and an archive left in would eventually push live notes out of the answer instead of merely padding it. `GET /api/files` is deliberately never filtered: that listing resolves a `[[wikilink]]`, and a link to an archived note reading as a dead one would make an empty second copy in `00 Inbox` out of a note the vault already holds. A move's link rewrite skips nothing either, an archived link being a link to rewrite. The status bar says when the archive is showing, a mode with nothing on screen saying so being the one you forget you are in.
* **Update**: startup writes `99 Misc/01 Config/01 Agents/How-To-Exam.md` beside the todo guide, each written on its own so a vault that kept one and deleted the other gets what it is missing. It is the exam format told as an instruction, for the agents that do not all live in the app.
* **Update**: a part takes the due date, the scheduled date, the start date and the priority off the todo above it where it spells none of its own, and anything it does spell wins. Everything else stays where it was written: `🆔` names one line, `➕ ✅ ❌` stamp what happened to it, `⏲` and `⏱` measure it, `⛔` waits and `🔁` copies, so passing any of them down would say of five lines what was written of one. It is read rather than written into the note, which is what lets a parent's date move every part with it and keeps the line saying only what somebody typed on it. Deliberately not folded into the tree the cascade tick reads, because that one writes its nodes back out and a filled field would land on a part's own line. [Subtasks](/reference/todo-format/state-changes.md#subtasks) says which fields come down and that the editor still draws each line as the note holds it, and [The todo pane](/reference/editor-keys/todo-pane.md#the-todo-pane) says an undated part sits in its parent's group and waits behind its parent's `🛫`. The agent note gained the same rule, told as an instruction: write the date once, on the parent.
* **Update**: the status bar names a failed write and a note changed on disk in words beside the warning sign, and hovering either says what to do about it: `:w` or Ctrl+S to send the text again, `:w` or `:e!` to settle a conflict. A failed write carries what the vault answered under it, `PUT /api/files/index.md failed with 500`, which is the difference between pressing `:w` again and going to look at the server. The sign on its own said something was wrong and nothing about which of the two it was or which key ends it. [Saving](/reference/editor-keys/saving.md#saving) covers the reading.
* **Update**: Enter follows the `[[link]]` under the cursor, beside `gf` and Ctrl+click, and off a link it still does what vim does with a bare `<CR>`, which is move to the first word of the next line. A note made by following a link that names nothing lands in `00 Inbox` where the target is a bare name; a target spelling a path out is still made at that path. A name says which note and never where it belongs, and the vault root is a worse guess than the folder that exists for notes with nowhere to be yet. The backend's copy of the resolution rule is unchanged: a rename follows a link to a note that exists, and the inbox is where a note is made rather than where one is looked for. [Wikilinks](/reference/editor-keys/links.md#wikilinks) carries the key and the rule, and [What a move does to the links](/reference/http-api/moving.md#what-a-move-does-to-the-links) says where the two rules part.
* **Creation**: Added [Importing a web page](/reference/editor-keys/capture.md#importing-a-web-page) to Editor keys and `GET /api/fetch` to [HTTP API](/reference/http-api/capture.md#get-apifetch). `<leader>cw` takes one address and puts the page in `00 Inbox` as a note, named after its title, headed by it, and carrying `source` and whatever the page says about its author and its date. The reading is defuddle, kepano's extractor and the one behind Obsidian's web clipper, running in the browser because it reads a DOM and that is where the DOM is; the backend fetches, because a script may ask another origin for a page and not read the answer. The endpoint takes http and https and nothing else, counts the bytes as they arrive rather than believing `content-length`, and names its refusal so the prompt can put it in front of the person who pasted the address. A page clipped twice opens the note it made the first time.
* **Creation**: Added [Deleting a note](/explanation/deleting-a-note.md), and `DELETE /api/files/{path}`, `DELETE /api/folders/{path}`, `GET /api/trash` and `PATCH /api/trash/{entry}` to [HTTP API](/reference/http-api.md). A delete moves the note into a hidden `.trash` inside the vault rather than removing it: the listing, rg, the event stream and the path resolver all refuse a hidden folder already, so the vault stops holding the note the moment it moves. The entry keeps the note's path with the moment appended to its leaf, which is the whole record, so a restore needs no list to read and a purge needs no timer file. A folder goes as one entry and comes back as one. It is not left to jj, which holds the note either way, because jj is optional and recovery cannot depend on a step a save is allowed to skip. `KASTEN_TRASH_DAYS` is thirty days and the purge runs at startup, which [Configuration](/reference/configuration.md) carries.
* **Update**: [Leader](/reference/editor-keys/leader.md#leader) gained `<leader>df`, which moves the open note into the trash, and `<leader>du`, which puts the last deleted note or folder back and opens it. [The file tree](/reference/editor-keys/file-tree.md#the-file-tree) gained `d`, which deletes the row under the cursor, folder included, the way its `r` renames one. Nothing asks first: the trash is the confirmation, and a dialog would cost a click on every delete to save one mistyped key. Text still waiting is written before the note goes, so the trash holds what you last typed, and every pane holding the note empties.

* **Creation**: Added [Books in the vault](/explanation/books-in-the-vault.md), `GET /api/assets/{path}` in [HTTP API](/reference/http-api/assets-and-books.md#get-apiassetspath) and [The book pane](/reference/editor-keys/book-pane.md#the-book-pane). `<leader>gr` opens the book that sits beside the open note in a pane to the right, and `h`, `l` and `q` turn the page and close it. A book is the note's path with the suffix swapped and nothing anywhere records the pair, so a folder move carries both and a rename of the note alone leaves the epub behind, which the reader says instead of reading on. Nothing else in the app knows the file is there: every reader of the vault already asks for `.md`, so the tree, the finder and search never mention it. Startup writes `vault/.gitignore` holding `*.epub` and `.*.tmp` before the guide, because jj tracks any untracked file under a megabyte and every save snapshots, so one note saved beside a book would put the book in the history for good; ignoring is not untracking, and `jj file untrack` is the way out for one already in there.
* **Update**: the app serves a Content Security Policy, written once in `frontend/src/lib/csp.ts` and read by both `nginx.conf` and a Vite plugin, with a test that fails when the two copies disagree. It is the whole defence against a script inside a book: foliate renders each chapter from a same-origin blob URL, a document loaded from one inherits the policy of the page that made it, and `script-src 'self'` refuses an inline script, one loaded from inside the archive and one loaded from anywhere else. Not the iframe sandbox, which foliate hard-codes behind closed shadow roots and kasten cannot reach. Development differs in one directive, a nonce for Vite's react-refresh preamble, minted at each server start rather than written down, because a book can carry a `nonce="…"` of its own.

* **Update**: reopening a book puts you back on the page you stopped at. The place is one line of the literature note's own frontmatter, `reading:`, holding the epubcfi foliate last reported and handed back to it when the book opens; delete the line and you lose a bookmark, which is what a bookmark is worth. The write waits a minute for the reading to stop, so two minutes of page turning is one jj change rather than forty, and it goes out at once when the reader closes. It is refused outright while the note sits in the pane you are typing into, dirty or not, and again after the read it makes, because `PUT` is last writer wins and a save landing between that read and the write would be overwritten by the older text plus one field. The pane listens on foliate's renderer rather than on its view, the view re-emitting the same event without the `reason` that tells a page turn from a re-render: without that, folding the tree or resizing the window would write a bookmark with nobody having moved. A bookmark left over from another edition opens a readable page either way it fails, a spine item the book has not got by navigating again with no location and a node path the chapter has not by catching the throw, which arrives from the book's own iframe and so is not `instanceof Error` in the window that catches it. [How it keeps your place](/explanation/books-in-the-vault/highlights-and-place.md#how-it-keeps-your-place) carries the three costs: the jj noise, the minute lost to a closed tab, and a save from an editor holding the note from before the bookmark landed dropping it until the next page turn. [Note frontmatter](/reference/note-frontmatter.md#every-other-field-is-yours) names `reading:` beside `source` as the second thing kasten writes into the half of the block that is yours, and [The book pane](/reference/editor-keys/book-pane.md#the-book-pane) says the key remembers where you were.
