The page carries the line and every field it can hold, in the spelling the obsidian-tasks plugin already reads, the five states and why there are five rather than two, the cycle and what each press stamps, the one order every line kasten writes carries, the `## Done` line and why it is deliberately not a checkbox, and the term language the pane's filter and the add prompt both read.
* **Update**: [HTTP API](/reference/http-api.md) gained `GET /api/todos`, which finds every line in the vault that could be a todo in one rg pass. The section says which two shapes it matches, why a wikilink bullet and an ordered list item cannot, why the done log line cannot either, why nothing here parses a todo, why the cap is 5,000 against search's 2,000, and that a `- [ ]` inside a fenced code block is a hit nothing can drop without reading the notes. The count at the head reads eleven endpoints.
* **Creation**: Added [Todos](/reference/editor-keys.md#todos), [The todo pane](/reference/editor-keys.md#the-todo-pane) and [The todo overlay](/reference/editor-keys.md#the-todo-overlay) to Editor keys. They say what `<leader>x` does to the line under the cursor and how the editor draws each state, what the pane groups on and what each of its keys does, why `/` is what focuses the filter line, why `d` groups on the day a todo was finished rather than on the day it was due, and why the overlay is the panel's backlinks mode rather than its search mode.
* **Update**: [Editor keys](/reference/editor-keys.md) gained `<leader>x`, `<leader>gt` and `<leader>ft` in the leader table. [Panes and tabs](/reference/editor-keys.md#panes-and-tabs) says a pane holds one note, one terminal or the todo list, that a tab holding the list is named `todos`, and that `<leader>q` takes the list out of a pane the way it takes a note out.
* **Update**: `<leader>x` writes the done log, not only the todo pane's `x`. Ticking a todo done in the note you are typing in is the way the design's fifth story words it, and it was the one path that left no `- ✅` line behind. The key still edits the buffer, so `u` undoes the line, and the log half is a write to another note that undo cannot reach. Only the two presses that enter or leave done read the vault at all. [Editor keys](/reference/editor-keys.md#todos) says so.
* **Update**: a todo living in today's own note is logged under `## Done` there like any other. The rule that skipped it read a note linking to itself as recording nothing, which is true of the link and not of the line, and the daily note is where most todos are written, so `## Done` stayed empty for the commonest way of working. `doneLine` already leaves the link off. The press that does it in the editor writes both halves in one transaction, so `u` takes back both.
* **Update**: the todo pane draws the year on a due date outside this one and cuts it from a date inside it. `Later` reaches years out, and two rows reading `01-01` said nothing about which was sooner.
* **Update**: [Todo format](/reference/todo-format.md) gained [Subtasks](/reference/todo-format.md#subtasks), [Dependencies](/reference/todo-format.md#dependencies) and [Recurrence](/reference/todo-format.md#recurrence). They say that nesting is the indent and nothing else, that prose between two todos ends no block, that a parent counts every descendant and ticking it ticks every open one for a single `- ✅` line, that `⛔` hands kasten the choice between `[ ]` and `[b]` on that line and touches no other state, that a dangling `⛔` and a hand-set `[b]` are both left alone, that a dependent opens only when every blocker on it closes, and what the fresh copy of a recurring todo keeps, drops and counts from.
* **Update**: [Todo format](/reference/todo-format.md) says `⏳`, `🛫`, `⛔` and `🔁` are read rather than carried, now that the first two decide which group a row sits in, the third decides the state of the line it sits on and the fourth writes the next copy. The id section says `<leader>i` stamps one as well as entering done.
* **Update**: [Editor keys](/reference/editor-keys.md) gained `<leader>i` in the leader table and `n` in the todo pane's. [Todos](/reference/editor-keys.md#todos) says one press can move a parent's parts, a recurrence copy and a dependent in another note, and that everything of that in this note comes back on one `u`. [The todo pane](/reference/editor-keys.md#the-todo-pane) says the group is read off the scheduled date, that a past due date wins, that a `🛫` after today keeps the row off the list, and what `n` shows.
* **Update**: a subtask in the todo pane is drawn one step in under the todo it belongs to. `phase2` on a row of its own said nothing about which thing it was phase two of, and the pane already knew the tree, that being what draws `2/6`. The indent is worked out inside the group rather than off the line's own spaces, because a part can land in a group its parent is not in: a part carrying a date of its own, or one whose parent is done, goes back to the edge and names its parent instead of hanging off whichever row happens to sit above it. [Editor keys](/reference/editor-keys.md#the-todo-pane) says so.
* **Update**: the cycle walks the work and nothing else, plain line to `[ ]` to `[/]` to `[x]` and out to a plain line, and blocked and rejected come off it onto five keys that set a state straight: `<leader>so`, `sp`, `sx`, `sb` and `sr` in the editor, `O P X B R` in the todo pane. The old order walked through done in the middle, and a row leaves the pane's list the moment it is done, so from there blocked and rejected could not be reached at all. A key that names a state stamps what the walk stamps and drags what it drags: a parent set to done still ticks its parts, a recurring todo still writes its next copy, and a blocker still moves what waits on it. [The cycle](/reference/todo-format.md#the-cycle) and [Setting a state](/reference/todo-format.md#setting-a-state) say so.
* **Update**: `GET /api/todos` carries a running session line and not a closed one. The endpoint answers one view, that view wants to know which rows have a timer going, and a closed session says nothing the task line does not already carry. The closed ones are also the only part of this feature that piles up, and they came back on every fetch. A stop reads them through `GET /api/search` on the id instead, one narrow pass at the press. The cap moved with it, from 5,000 to 100,000: 5,000 was a judgment about how much work a person will read doing a safety limit's job, and reaching it truncated the list with nothing on screen to say so. [HTTP API](/reference/http-api.md#get-apitodos) says both.
* **Creation**: Added [The time log](/reference/todo-format.md#the-time-log) to Todo format. `t` in the todo pane starts a session on the todo under the cursor and writes it under `## Time` in today's daily note; a second press closes every session that todo has running and rewrites its `⏱`. The section carries both spellings of a session line, that timers run in parallel and a day's total is the sum of its intervals rather than the wall clock they span, that the log is the record and `⏱` is kasten's summary of it rewritten at every stop, that a session is closed in the note it lives in at 23:59 when that note is not today's, and what that costs a session that ran past midnight.
* **Update**: [Todo format](/reference/todo-format.md) counts nine fields written by kasten rather than seven. The estimate and the worked total moved out of the list you type yourself: `est:45m` in the add prompt writes the `⏲` and every stop of the timer writes the `⏱`. The page also says how a duration is spelled, and the block anchor paragraph says the time log names the id rather than that it will.
* **Update**: [The todo pane](/reference/editor-keys.md#the-todo-pane) gained `t`, the `▶` a row carries while a timer is going on it, the day beside that mark where the session was opened on an earlier one, the running count in the footer, and the cell that draws the worked total against the estimate. The pane shows what is on disk: there is no clock ticking on a row, and a session lands in `⏱` when it is stopped.
* **Creation**: Added [Saved views](/reference/todo-format.md#saved-views) to Todo format. `99 Misc/01 Config/todo-views.md` holds named filters, one per list item, read by the same terms the filter line takes. The section carries the three defaults kasten writes, that the name runs up to the first colon so a filter carrying one of its own splits where it should, and that a line the parser cannot read is skipped and takes no other line with it.
* **Update**: [The todo pane](/reference/editor-keys.md#the-todo-pane) gained `v`, which walks the saved views one press at a time. The terms of the view showing sit in the filter line where they can be edited, its name sits in the header, one more press past the last gives the whole list back, and typing over the terms clears the name. The first press in a vault holding no views note writes it with three defaults, the way the periodic keys make the note they open.
* **Update**: [The todo pane](/reference/editor-keys.md#the-todo-pane) gained `i`, vim's own key for starting to type where the cursor is, which turns the row under the cursor into the line it was read from, markdown and emoji as the note holds them, and writes back what you leave in it. Nothing is expanded: the line is the whole record, so an edit is the line itself, and that is the one path to `🔁`, `⏳` and the indent that makes a todo a part of another. The whole row gives way to the input because the whole line is what is edited, what the row draws being a reading of it. The list's keys are the input's while it is open, Escape writes nothing and an empty input writes nothing either. The note is read again first and the old line checked against the row, so an edit onto a stale row writes nothing rather than replacing whatever now sits at that number. The add prompt hands the keys back to the pane on the way out, which a row redrawn under it used to lose.
* **Update**: [The todo pane](/reference/editor-keys.md#the-todo-pane) gained `s`, which opens the add prompt on the row under the cursor and puts what you type in as a part of that todo: in the note that todo lives in, one step further in, after the parts it already has. The prompt is the same prompt and reads the same shorthand, so it says `part` in the header and names the todo it is going under, and the note is read again first with the parent's line checked against the row, the way `i` checks it. [Subtasks](/reference/todo-format.md#subtasks) says the indent is written for you from the pane as well as typed by hand.
* **Creation**: Added [Completing a todo field](/reference/editor-keys.md#completing-a-todo-field) to Editor keys and [Writing a field](/reference/todo-format.md#writing-a-field) to Todo format. The pages say why `➕`, `✅`, `❌` and `🆔` are not on the list, that Ctrl+Space opens it without the colon, and that the todo pane's `i` draws the same fields as buttons under the line it is editing, reached by Tab or by a click.

* **Creation**: Added [The agent note](/reference/todo-format.md#the-agent-note) to Todo format. The backend writes `99 Misc/01 Config/01 Agents/How-To-TODO.md` into a vault holding none, at startup rather than on a key press, because the agents that read it are not all inside the app. It carries the fields and their values, the states, the field order, the recurrence rules, the subtask indent and the two `rg` lines that read a vault, and the rule that a todo is edited in the markdown while ticking one done goes through `PUT /api/files/{path}`, with every write a completion is spelled out. The shell container gained `KASTEN_API`, `curl` and `jq` so an agent in a terminal pane can send that.
* **Update**: [Live preview and the vim mode](/explanation/live-preview.md) gained [The gutter counts from the cursor](/explanation/live-preview.md#the-gutter-counts-from-the-cursor). The editor's line numbers are vim's `number relativenumber`: the cursor's line names itself, every other line carries its distance from it, and the count `10j` or `d5k` wants can be read off rather than counted. The section also records that the gutter redraws because `highlightActiveLineGutter` moves its marker when the cursor changes line, which is what would break if that extension were dropped.
* **Update**: [Formatting](/reference/editor-keys.md#formatting) gained `<leader>=`, which tidies the whole note: trailing whitespace off every line, a run of blank lines down to one, a blank line in front of a heading that has none, and `*` and `+` bullets written as `-`. The frontmatter is YAML and a fence holds whatever it holds, so both are stepped over. It writes one change per line rather than replacing the note, which is what keeps the cursor where it was. `=` is vim's own key for putting the formatting right, `gq` being spent on the quarter's note.
* **Update**: [The todo pane](/reference/editor-keys.md#the-todo-pane) sorts each group by priority, highest first and no priority ranking above low, the way obsidian-tasks reads a bare line. Grouping by date said which day to act on a row and nothing about which row to take first, so a busy group was whatever order the vault answered in. What sorts is the run a row heads rather than the row, so a part travels with the todo it hangs off however low its own priority is, and a tie keeps the vault's order.
