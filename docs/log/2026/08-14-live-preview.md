# 2026-08-14: the editor

* **Update**: the editor tells its inline constructs apart by colour, and it draws a tag. `#kasten` is parsed rather than matched, a small inline extension beside the one reading `==highlighted==`, because a regex over the text would colour the hash in `#!/bin/sh` inside a fence, in a URL's `#anchor` and in `issue#12`, while an inline parser is never asked about a range a code span, a fence or an autolink has already taken. Nothing about a tag is hidden: the hash is part of the word rather than a mark standing in front of one, so the tag reads the same in normal mode and in insert mode, the way an overdue date does. It is drawn as a purple pill everywhere but in a table, where the padding would push the wall to its right out of line with every wall above it and the colour has to carry it alone. Bold and italic gained colours too, bold brightening off the body colour and italic taking the orange, both being easy to miss and easy to confuse with each other once the asterisks are off the screen. [A tag has no mark to hide](/explanation/live-preview.md#a-tag-has-no-mark-to-hide) says why the parser and not the regex, and [Colour tells the inline constructs apart](/explanation/live-preview.md#colour-tells-the-inline-constructs-apart) carries the table of what each one wears.
* **Update**: a table is drawn as a table. Where live preview took the pipes and made them read as one, a block widget now replaces the whole run with a real `<table>`, head row, borders and each column's text on the side its dashes ask for, `alignsOf` in `table.ts` reading that off the row of dashes so the drawing and `<leader>=` share one idea of it. The cells are cut out of the syntax tree rather than out of the text, so a cell wears the colours a paragraph wears and a tag in one gets back the pill it loses in the source, where padding would push the wall to its right out of line. The columns are counted off the walls and not off the cells, an empty cell being no node at all: `| a |  | c |` read by its cells is two columns and read by its walls is the three it says. Two rules here are the table's own. The block flips whole rather than a line at a time, half a table drawn and half of it in pipes being neither, and the cursor decides rather than the mode: `j` and `k` move by what is on the screen, and a block widget is one thing to CodeMirror rather than the five lines it stands for, so both step clean over a table and a rule waiting for insert mode would leave one nothing could open. What reaches in moves by document position, `w`, a search, a line jump, and a click, the widget taking mouse events rather than swallowing them. A read-only view never reveals, its selection sitting at offset zero because nothing moved it, so the finder, search and the review pane draw a note that opens with a table rather than a pane of pipes; `EditorState.readOnly` is what tells them from the editor, `EditorView.editable` being view-level and invisible to a state field. The spacing above and below the grid is padding on a wrapper rather than a margin on the table, a block widget being measured by the box it hands back, and a margin left the gutter counting lines against a height short by it. The review pane draws both sides of a card through the same rendering, a card holding a table having been a row of pipes until now. [The three blocks](/explanation/live-preview.md#the-three-blocks) says why the cursor decides there and what a pane with no cursor does, and [Tables](/reference/editor-keys.md#tables) says what you see and which keys go in.
* **Update**: a fenced code block hides its backticks and its language, the shaded surface saying where the block starts and ends better than they did. Each fence line goes whole, with the line break that keeps it off the code, so the block ends on a line of code rather than on a blank row. That is the one hidden run in the rendering that crosses a line break, and CodeMirror draws the pair it joins as one line wearing the upper one's class, so the bottom of the surface hangs on the last line of code and, in a block of a single line, on the opening fence. The cursor anywhere in the block hands the fences back rather than the cursor's line the way every other construct works, a hidden fence line being a line the cursor cannot reach, and a block with no code between its fences keeps them: hiding both would leave no line on the screen to put the cursor back on, and that is the block a third backtick writes. [The three blocks](/explanation/live-preview.md#the-three-blocks) says why the fences leave with their line breaks, and [Backticks](/reference/editor-keys.md#backticks) says what you see and how to get the source back.
* **Update**: an inline construct shows its marks only while the cursor is inside it. Insert mode revealed the whole of the cursor's line, so editing one word put the asterisks, tildes and brackets of every other construct on that line back on the screen, which is the clutter the rendering exists to take away. Bold, italic, strikethrough, inline code, a highlight, a link and a wikilink now go by the node; a heading, a bullet, a todo's box and a blockquote's bar keep the line rule, their mark sitting at the head of the line so the two rules say the same thing there. It also settled a disagreement nobody had noticed: `- [/] task` parses its box as a link, and a revealed line handed the box back through the bullet while the link rendering hid its brackets, so the link rendering now stands down where a todo's box begins. [The mode decides, not the cursor](/explanation/live-preview.md#the-mode-decides-not-the-cursor) carries the split, and [Formatting](/reference/editor-keys.md#formatting) says which constructs answer to which rule.
* **Update**: a table ends where its rows do, and the lines of a note stand further apart. GFM ends a table at a blank line, so a paragraph written straight under one is parsed as rows of a single cell, and the drawn table swallowed two lines of prose into its first column. The rendering now reads the end itself: a row is a line carrying a `|`, the same reading `tableAt` walks a table by, the first line without one closes the table, and the widget covers the rows alone while the walk goes on into the prose below and renders it as prose, inline code and all. The cursor test moved to that end too, so typing under a table no longer flips the table back to pipes. Beside it every line of the document gained a little padding under it, which is spacing between the lines somebody typed and costs a wrapped line nothing, CodeMirror giving each line of the document one element however many rows it draws as. A fence and a table's source are left tight, both being one surface rather than a run of lines. Pressing Enter and pressing Shift+Enter are the same key here, both writing one newline, so both get the space. [The three blocks](/explanation/live-preview.md#the-three-blocks) says where a table stops and why the parser is not asked.
* **Update**: type a `#` and the vault's tags are offered. A new `GET /api/tags` is one rg pass matching the tags themselves rather than the lines holding them, deduplicated and sorted, and the pattern is `tag.ts`'s own, so nothing is offered that the editor would not then colour as a tag: `#!/bin/sh`, `#anchor` in a URL and `note#2` are on nobody's list. The leading character in it is consumed rather than looked behind, rg's engine having no lookbehind, and cut off again on this side, which is why it is taken from the last hash and not the first. The completion joins the three `basicSetup` already mounts, on the markdown language beside the wikilink's and the todo's, and the whole vocabulary goes in every time for the reason the whole vault does behind a `[[`: CodeMirror scores the labels and keeps the best, and the labels carry their hash so `#db` is scored against `#dbt` whole. A hash at the start of a line waits for a letter, that being where a heading is opened and `#` alone saying nothing about which of the two was meant, while `#flashcards/aws` on a line of its own is the vault's own convention and is what makes refusing the line outright wrong. The archive is in the answer, alone among the scans: what is filed away is out of a search because it is not what is being looked for, and a tag is a word you are trying to spell the way you spelled it last time. The list is read again on every write rather than on a `listing`, because a tag is a word inside a note and the writes that change the vocabulary are exactly the ones the route's early return walks past. [Completing a tag](/reference/editor-keys.md#completing-a-tag) says what the keys do and [GET /api/tags](/reference/http-api.md#get-apitags) states the endpoint.
* **Update**: `<leader>gv` plays a note's video in a pane beside the note. It follows `<leader>gr` down to the reuse of the pane already open, because the two keys do the same job: put the thing the note is about next to the note about it. A pane and not an embed drawn into the text, which is the whole decision here. An embed scrolls away the moment you write three lines under it, and it takes the keyboard as soon as you click it, so the note it belongs to becomes the one place you cannot type; a pane keeps its place while the note scrolls, and every key that already moves between panes, closes one or splits one works on it with nothing added. The link is read out of the note and the first YouTube address in it wins, the pane reading its own note the way the exam pane does, so the query is the editor's own and opening the player beside a note on screen fetches nothing. The first and not the one under the cursor: a note is about one video, and reading the cursor would restart the player on every line moved. `frame-src` gained `https://www.youtube.com` beside the reader's `blob:`, one named host rather than the whole of `https:`, and `www.youtube.com` rather than the nocookie mirror, the privacy that host buys being already yours behind oauth2-proxy while the extensions worth having in a player, the blocker and the sponsor skipper, name the ordinary host first. Two things are left where they are. A click into the player is not heard, a cross-origin frame telling its parent nothing and the window-blur trick that guesses at it being more machinery than a border on the wrong pane is worth; and `?t=90` is dropped, a link to a moment opening at the start. [The video pane](/reference/editor-keys.md#the-video-pane) says what the key does and what the frame will and will not answer.
* **Update**: a note's YouTube link is drawn as the player where it sits. `![](https://youtu.be/iDulhoQ2pro)` stayed as its source, `imageSource` answering null for every address outside the vault; the same brackets now draw a frame, `videoId` asking `noteVideos` about a single URL so the embed and the pane read the same address shapes and neither can learn one the other does not. The pane stays the way to watch something properly, this being the other half of it: a clip quoted mid-note reads as the clip. It opens at the position the note remembers and writes none back, so `watching:` still moves for the pane's player alone. The widget compares by video id and not by its URL, which matters here and for no other widget in the file: the position is written into the note while you watch, so a URL comparison would call every write a new widget and remount the frame under a playing video. The ceiling is the reveal rule every construct here follows, the cursor on the line handing the source back and taking the frame with it, which is exactly the objection the pane was built on and is why the pane is still there. [The video pane](/reference/editor-keys.md#in-the-note-itself) says what the brackets draw and [Live preview and the vim mode](/explanation/live-preview.md) says why the id is what it compares.
