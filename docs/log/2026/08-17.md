# 2026-08-17

* **Update**: a figure taken out of a book lands in the vault beside the passage. A drag over a plate and its caption used to report the caption alone, and the picture, which is half of what a technical book says, stayed inside the epub where nothing but the reader could reach it. The pane now reads the selection for an `<img>` and files it: `cloneContents().querySelector("img")` so a figure two paragraphs down the page cannot come along with a run of text that never touched it, `fetch` on the `blob:` URL foliate rewrote the resource to, which is created in the main frame (`epub.js:727`) and therefore reachable from the pane, and `uploadAsset` into the folder a pasted screenshot goes to under the name a paste gives one. The bytes are the book's own rather than a canvas re-encode of whatever fitted on the page, and the media type comes off the same response, which is what lets the vault's magic check pass. The block gains one `![](path)` line above the quote, because a caption sits under its figure, and a plain markdown image rather than an embed, `![[...]]` being a syntax kasten's editor does not draw. The empty-text guard had to go: `selection.toString()` answers nothing for a drag over a picture, so a plate selected on its own was indistinguishable from letting go of a selection, and the two are now told apart by whether the range holds an image. The upload goes first and the block is written when it lands, the rule the paste settled, so a vault that refuses the file leaves the note as it was and says why in the status bar rather than writing a reference to nothing; an `.svg` plate is that case, `ASSET_MAGIC` holding png, jpeg, gif and webp and no vector format, and a book whose figures are all SVG gets nothing until a row is added there. The name is now `filedName` and exported from `image.ts`, one naming rule for everything that lands in the images folder rather than a second one for the reader. [Highlight format](/reference/highlight-format.md#the-figure) states the line and [The book pane](/reference/editor-keys.md#the-book-pane) says what the key takes.

[HTTP API](/reference/http-api.md#get-apicards) lists the five shapes the scan matches.
* **Update**: a card can be parked. `!suspended` beside the schedule takes one card out of the queue without moving its note, and a question with no answer under it, `What is a moved block?::`, is parked by its shape rather than dropped. Both readers agree on both shapes now, which is the point of the change and not a side effect of it: `srs.ts` has always skipped a card with an empty back and `review.ts` counted it, so the overview offered cards the sitting would never ask and there was no way to see which. The rule they share lives in `card-line.ts`, which gained `suspended`, `parks` and `divide` and now owns the schedule comment's pattern too, one expression rather than one per reader. A parked card stays in `parseCards`' output carrying `parked`, because the queue addresses a card by its ordinal (`review-session.tsx:82`) and dropping one would renumber the cards behind it in a sitting already running. A note reviewed whole has no line to mark, so `sr-suspended: true` in its frontmatter parks it beside the schedule it already keeps there, and putting it back writes `false` rather than deleting the key. The overview counts the parked ones on the row and in the header, summed over top-level rows the way `to go` is, because a deck of nothing but parked cards lands disabled and a bare `0 new` would leave no way to find out why. [Flashcard format](/reference/flashcard-format.md#parking-a-card) states both shapes, [Note frontmatter](/reference/note-frontmatter.md) the field, and [Spaced repetition](/explanation/spaced-repetition.md#archiving-is-the-archive) says why a per-card state now sits beside the folder.
* **Update**: the review pane gained a third screen and two keys over the card in front of you. `s` parks the card on screen and moves on without rating it, before the answer is shown as well as after, because a card you want out of the deck is one you recognised from its question alone; `setSuspended` splices the token and leaves the three numbers byte-identical, so putting the card back returns it on the date it already held. `n` opens a line for a question the card just reminded you of, and what you type lands at the end of that card's note as `Question::`. The end and never the middle: a card appended is last in line order, so every ordinal the running queue holds still addresses the card it did, which is the rule `review-session.tsx:82` states and the reason capture appends rather than inserts. `p` opens the parked list, where `j` and `k` walk the rows, `u` puts one back and `o` opens its note. The pane's one nullable deck became a three-way `Screen`, because a third screen reached with a null deck would have silently inherited the overview's keys, and `routes/review.tsx` carries the same switch so the phone has all three screens too, its way onto the parked list being the header's `N parked` button rather than a key it cannot press. One regression fixed along the way and one found in the browser rather than in a test: the pane's keys now stand down while an `input` or `textarea` has the cursor, so a `1` typed into a question is a digit and not a rating (a space in the typing field used to reveal the answer, too), `u` returns the focus to the pane, the row it was on having gone with the card, and `l` does the same, the deck row it pressed going with the overview: every key of a sitting started from the keyboard used to reach nothing until you clicked the pane, which is why `s` and `n` were unreachable at a desk in the one flow that matters.
